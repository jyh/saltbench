
# How work is done in this repository
These are the standing practices for work here. They say how a change is expected to be made
and what counts as finished; they do not say how to solve any particular problem.

1. Nothing is finished until the checker says so. Compile before you claim anything, and read
   the output. A thing that does not compile is not done, and neither is a thing you have not run.
2. No claim without its checker. A placeholder — `sorry`, `admit`, an axiom you introduce, a
   decision procedure standing in for an argument — is not a proof; it is a record that the work
   is unfinished. Do not leave one where a proof belongs. If you cannot finish, say so plainly in
   your final message and say what defeated you.
3. Statements are never weakened to admit a proof. If a statement looks wrong, too strong or
   unprovable, that is a finding to report, not a thing to edit. Working under it as written is
   the job; changing it is not yours to do.
4. A check that cannot fail is not a check. When something passes, make it fail on purpose once —
   break a hypothesis or a helper so the goal is false, confirm the failure, then put it back.
   Only then believe the pass.
5. Say again in simpler terms what you established, and derive it rather than asserting it: a
   short restatement proved as a consequence of what you landed is worth more than a comment
   claiming the same thing.
6. Attempts are budgeted and exhaustion is reported, not ground through. Work in a few serious
   attempts; when one fails, name what failed and change route rather than repeating it. A
   recorded dead end is a result.
7. Try to break a candidate before you accept it, and settle beforehand what would count as done,
   so the bar is never written after the fact.
8. Keep the record honest: in your final message list the decisions you made and the ones you
   retracted, errors included.
